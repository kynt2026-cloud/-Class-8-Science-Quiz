Question 1 — Basic Unit of Life
A researcher removes a few cells from a leaf and places them in a nutrient medium. The cells remain alive for some time and perform respiration, exchange gases, and respond to stimuli. Which conclusion is most appropriate?
  • A. A leaf is the basic unit of life.
  • B. A cell can carry out several life processes independently.
  • C. Tissues can survive without cells.
  • D. Organelles are independent living units.
Correct Answer: B
The observation shows that an individual cell can perform essential life processes such as respiration, gas exchange, and response to stimuli. This proves that the cell is the smallest structural and functional unit capable of carrying out the activities of life.
Question 2 — Cellular Powerhouse
Two types of human cells are examined under a microscope. Cell P contains very few mitochondria, while Cell Q contains numerous mitochondria. Which statement is most likely correct?
  • A. Cell P requires more energy than Cell Q.
  • B. Cell Q performs more energy-demanding activities.
  • C. Cell P can carry out photosynthesis.
  • D. Cell Q lacks a nucleus.
Correct Answer: B
Cells that need more energy usually contain more mitochondria because mitochondria release ATP during cellular respiration.
Question 3 — Plant vs Animal Cell
A student observes a cell with a rigid outer boundary and a large central vacuole, but no chloroplasts are visible. Which statement is correct?
  • A. It must be an animal cell.
  • B. It cannot be a plant cell.
  • C. It is most likely a plant cell from a non-green part of a plant.
  • D. It is a bacterial cell.
Correct Answer: C
The rigid boundary indicates a cell wall, and the large vacuole is typical of plant cells. Non-green plant parts such as roots may not contain visible chloroplasts.
Question 4 — Assertion & Reason
Assertion (A): A plant cell placed in distilled water becomes turgid but does not burst.

Reason (R): The rigid cell wall develops an opposing pressure that balances the inward movement of water.
  • A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  • B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  • C. A is true, but R is false.
  • D. A is false, but R is true.
Correct Answer: A
Water enters the cell by osmosis, increasing turgidity. The cell wall exerts wall pressure that prevents the cell from bursting.
Question 5 — Data Interpretation
A student studies three cells and records the following observations.
Cell Observation
P Many mitochondria, no cell wall
Q Cell wall present, large vacuole, no chloroplasts
R No nucleus, no mitochondria, ribosomes present
Which option correctly identifies the cells?
  • A. P – Plant cell; Q – Animal cell; R – Bacterial cell
  • B. P – Animal cell; Q – Plant cell; R – Bacterial cell
  • C. P – Animal cell; Q – Plant cell; R – Fungal cell
  • D. P – Muscle cell; Q – Leaf cell; R – Virus
Correct Answer: B
P lacks a cell wall and has many mitochondria, so it is an animal cell. Q has a cell wall and large vacuole, indicating a plant cell from a non-green part. R lacks a nucleus but has ribosomes, so it is a bacterial cell.
Question 6 — High Olympiad Challenge
A cell contains a plasma membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, and circular DNA. It lacks a nuclear membrane, mitochondria, and chloroplasts. Which statement is most accurate?
  • A. It cannot synthesize proteins because it lacks mitochondria.
  • B. It is a prokaryotic cell capable of independent life processes.
  • C. It is an animal cell in an early stage of development.
  • D. It is a virus because it lacks a nucleus.
Correct Answer: B
The presence of ribosomes and circular DNA without a nuclear membrane indicates a prokaryotic cell, such as a bacterium, which can perform essential life processes independently.
Summary & Key Takeaways
  • Cell: Smallest structural and functional unit of life.
  • Mitochondria: Site of ATP production during cellular respiration.
  • Cell Wall: Provides rigidity and prevents bursting in plant cells.
  • Large Vacuole: Maintains turgidity in plant cells.
  • Prokaryotes: Lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
  • Non-green plant cells: May have a cell wall and vacuole but no visible chloroplasts.
